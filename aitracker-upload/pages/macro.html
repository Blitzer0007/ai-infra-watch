Snapshot: the Iran conflict and markets
As of late June 2026
snapshot, not live

A U.S.–Iran conflict that began in spring 2026 has repeatedly moved global equity and oil markets. An April 8 ceasefire briefly lifted the S&P 500 to record highs on hopes for lower energy costs, but the truce has been fragile — Iran suspended negotiations on June 1, and both sides have since traded accusations of ceasefire violations, including reported attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil normally flows. Commercial shipping through the Strait has reportedly remained near a standstill despite the ceasefire holding on paper.

Markets have whipsawed on the president's own statements — equities have dropped on threats of renewed strikes and rallied on signals that a deal was close. Oil-price sensitivity is the main transmission channel into equities: higher energy costs raise inflation expectations, which raises the odds the Fed holds or hikes rates rather than cutting, which pressures growth and rate-sensitive tech and AI infrastructure names.

How this connects to the AI infra trade
Rates channel

Higher oil → higher inflation → fewer/later rate cuts. Long-duration growth names (Nebius, unprofitable neoclouds) are more rate-sensitive than cash-generative mega-caps like NVIDIA.

Capex channel

Hyperscaler AI capex guidance (Meta's $115–135B, etc.) is a separate, more durable driver than daily headlines — but a genuine growth scare could make boards pause spending.

Risk-appetite channel

High-multiple, high-growth, pre-profit names like Nebius tend to sell off hardest in broad risk-off days, regardless of company-specific news — worth separating "the stock fell because of the war" from "the stock fell because of its own news."
