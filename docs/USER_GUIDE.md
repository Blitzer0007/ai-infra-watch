# AI Infra Watch — Plain-English User Guide

## 1. What is AI Infra Watch?

AI Infra Watch is a research and monitoring dashboard for an AI-infrastructure portfolio.

It connects:
- live market prices
- portfolio holdings
- contracts and business catalysts
- historical event/price reactions
- macro and geopolitical risks
- policy signals
- congressional trading disclosures
- peer and company relationships
- autonomous AI research

The goal is to answer:
1. What is happening to my holdings?
2. What evidence explains it?
3. What external events could affect them?
4. How did the market react to similar events before?
5. What deserves my attention next?

Important: model outputs are research signals, not automatic buy/sell instructions.

## 4. Portfolio Intelligence — understand your holdings

Portfolio Intelligence is the main portfolio analysis layer.

It combines:
- holdings
- live quotes
- group and peer performance
- catalysts
- contracts
- news/evidence
- macro risks
- event studies
- relationship/network data

### Invested Cost
Recorded capital cost of the position.

Example:
Invested cost = $1,000.

### Live Current
Estimated current value using the latest available quote.

Example:
10 shares × $120 = $1,200.

### AI Infrastructure Signal
A model-derived signal based on observed market/group inputs.

A score of 70/100 does NOT mean a 70% probability of a price increase.

### Position State

ADD REVIEW:
Investigate supporting or potentially positive evidence. It does not mean buy.

RISK REVIEW:
Investigate downside or thesis-risk evidence. It does not mean sell.

INSUFFICIENT DATA:
There is not enough evidence for a strong conclusion.

## 5. Portfolio Triage — what deserves attention first?

Triage means review priority.

It answers:

Which holdings currently deserve investigation, and why?

Example:

MU → RISK REVIEW

Possible reasons:
- semiconductor exposure
- new supply-chain evidence
- negative market move
- relevant macro risk

Correct workflow:

1. Open MU.
2. Read the reason.
3. Open the evidence.
4. Check whether the source is primary or secondary.
5. Check historical event reactions.
6. Decide what the evidence means for your own thesis.

Never treat the state label by itself as a trading instruction.

## 6. Evidence — why does the application say this?

Evidence is the underlying information supporting a signal.

Examples:
- SEC filings
- company investor-relations releases
- government announcements
- official contracts
- earnings information
- market prices
- news reports
- political/policy statements
- historical events

### Primary evidence
Original or official source.

Examples:
SEC filing, company release, government announcement.

Use it as the preferred verification source.

### Secondary evidence
A report describing the original event.

Useful for context, but verify important claims against the primary source where possible.

### Model-derived signal
A calculation produced by the application from multiple inputs.

It is a research output, not a source itself.

### How to read evidence

Ask:
1. What happened?
2. Who says it?
3. When did it happen?
4. Is the source primary?
5. Which company is affected?
6. What is the transmission mechanism?
7. What does the evidence NOT prove?

Example:

Evidence: A company announced a new AI data-center agreement.

Interpretation: The agreement may create future demand for its products.

What it does NOT prove: The stock must rise.

## 7. Contracts Ledger — business catalysts

Contracts Ledger tracks commercial agreements and infrastructure catalysts.

Look for:
- company
- customer
- contract/catalyst
- value, when disclosed
- date
- status
- evidence/source

Interpretation:

Signed contract = documented commercial agreement.

Planned project = evidence of intent, but execution is still future-dependent.

Milestone/progress = evidence that execution has moved forward.

Missing contract value does not mean zero value; it may simply be undisclosed.

Example:

Company announces a major AI data-center agreement.

Workflow:
Contract → verify source → check scope → check timing → check revenue implications → check market reaction.

## 8. Progress Tracker — event versus price

Progress Tracker connects company milestones with stock-price history.

The key concept is:

Event → date → price reaction → persistence.

Example:

Day 0: AI contract announced at $100.
Day +1: $108.
Day +5: $105.
Day +30: $112.

Interpretation:
The market initially reacted positively, partially reversed, then later traded above the event price.

This does not prove the contract caused the entire move. Other market factors may have contributed.

Green milestone dots can be clicked to read event briefs and valuation/price context.

## 9. Event Study — historical market reaction

Event Study asks:

What happened to this stock around similar historical events?

Look for:
- immediate reaction
- multi-day reaction
- persistence
- reversals
- broader market context

Example:

Five historical announcements were followed by positive short-term reactions.

Meaning:
There is historical evidence of a pattern.

It does NOT mean the next announcement will produce the same result.

### Portfolio Exposure Matrix

Direct:
Evidence indicates a direct relationship.

Secondary:
The relationship exists through another company, supplier, customer, geography or market.

Limited:
Current evidence shows limited connection.

Important:
No evidence of direct exposure does not prove that no risk exists. The application should not create a direct exposure classification without supporting evidence.

## 11. Scenario Sensitivity — what if a risk gets worse?

Scenario Sensitivity is not a price forecast.

Inputs may include:
- Taiwan disruption
- power-grid shortfall
- export-control breadth

The result shows modeled portfolio sensitivity under those assumptions.

Example:

Taiwan disruption = 70
Power risk = 30
Export controls = 40

Interpretation:

Under these assumptions, which holdings have greater modeled exposure?

Not:

The portfolio will fall by a specific percentage.

## 15. AI Research — autonomous investigation

AI Research is the autonomous research layer.

Good questions:

What new evidence from the last 24 hours could materially affect my AI-infrastructure holdings?

Which holdings have new contract evidence?

What new AI policy signals affect my holdings?

Which recent events had unusual price reactions?

Weak question:

Which stock will go up tomorrow?

AI Research is most useful when it collects, connects and explains evidence rather than pretending to know the future.

### Step 4 — Check Contracts

- customer commitments

### Step 5 — Check Macro
Review:
- export controls
- Taiwan exposure

### Step 7 — Form your own conclusion

Current state
+ evidence

# 20. Vocabulary

Signal = measurable indication that something changed.

Evidence = information supporting a signal.

Primary source = original/official source.

Secondary source = reporting about the original event.

Catalyst = event that could materially affect a company.

Exposure = how strongly a company is connected to an event or risk.

Direct = direct relationship supported by evidence.

Secondary = indirect relationship through another layer.

Limited = currently limited evidence of exposure.

Triage = decide what deserves attention first.

Risk Review = investigate relevant downside/risk evidence.

Add Review = investigate supporting/positive evidence.

Insufficient Data = not enough evidence for a strong conclusion.

Event Study = historical event versus market-reaction analysis.

Scenario Sensitivity = modeled exposure under scenario assumptions.

Portfolio Weight = holding's share of portfolio capital.

Transmission Chain = event → business effect → market/peer effect.

Live Current = current value based on the latest available quote.

AI Infra Signal = model-derived infrastructure signal.

Rotation = relative movement between tracked groups.

Network = relationships between companies/events.

# 21. Color guide

Green:
Supporting or positive observed condition. Read the evidence.

Amber:
Watch, uncertainty or change. Investigate further.

Red:
Risk/review condition. Investigate the underlying evidence.

Grey:
Neutral, unavailable or insufficient evidence.

Always read the accompanying text and source; do not interpret color alone.

# 22. Evidence → Interpretation → Action

For every important item use three steps.

Evidence:
What do we know?

Interpretation:
Why could it matter?

Action:
What should I personally investigate?

Example:

Evidence:
Company announces a large AI data-center agreement.

Interpretation:
The agreement could increase demand for its infrastructure products.

Action:
Check customer, contract duration, revenue timing, capacity requirements and historical market reaction.

This keeps AI Infra Watch focused on research rather than automatic investment instructions.
